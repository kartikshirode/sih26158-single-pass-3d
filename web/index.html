Tesseract turns a single drone flight into a textured 3D model on one laptop, with no second pass and no ground survey. Left of the line is the drone's own photo. Right of it is the model, seen from exactly where the drone was. Drag the line to compare.

Held-out check: the model is rendered into photos it never used, and scored against them.

The pass

The problem statement asks for something drone mapping usually avoids: one flight, one angle, and a model accurate enough to measure. Survey workflows fly a grid, twice, with markers on the ground. Disaster response, border mapping and reconnaissance rarely get that chance.

The demo clip is 18.9 s of a consumer drone gliding over a construction site. Tesseract keeps 177 sharp, well-spaced frames of the 568, works out where the camera was for each one, and builds 210,307 textured triangles from them in 264 s.

The clip carries no GPS, so this model is in its own units until one known length sets the scale. Clips with a DJI telemetry file come out in metres and on the map.

The site

Method

Every stage writes what it did and how long it took. These are the demo run's own timings on an RTX 4060 laptop.

  1. ScreenRefuse or repair a clip before spending time on it: horizon, sky, overlays, cuts.1.7 s
  2. IngestScore every frame for blur and motion; keep a sharp, evenly spaced set.4.1 s
  3. CameraMapAnything reads the lens from 60 frames; no calibration needed.33.8 s
  4. PosesCOLMAP matches features and solves where every frame was taken.70.4 s
  5. DepthOpenMVS measures depth for every pixel on the GPU and fuses it.53.6 s
  6. SurfaceA mesh through the fused points.37.7 s
  7. TextureEach face takes the best photo of it; unseen faces take the cloud's colour.52.0 s
  8. Level and exportGravity up, then OBJ, GLB, FBX, PLY, LAS and GeoTIFF.8.2 s

Limits

  • One angle. A single pass sees the far field only at a grazing angle, and never sees the back of anything. That's physics, not software.
  • The sky crop. Frames with a horizon lose their top third to keep the sky out of the depth maps, and with it most of the distant houses.
  • Seams. Each patch of texture comes from a different photo, so exposure changes can show at patch borders.
  • Scale. Without GPS, or a gimbal angle in the telemetry, heights are only as good as the lens estimate.
We'd rather show these than hide them. The workspace labels every number with where its scale came from.
