# Gallery: every clip we reconstructed

Source drone video on the left, the interactive 3D model on the right, for all three
runs that reached a 3D model. Where a clip was carried through the full pipeline, the
**feed-forward baseline and the MVS rebuild sit on one toggle** in the same metric
frame, so switching between them compares geometry rather than normalisation.

Open `index.html`. Keep `mesh/` and `assets/` beside it.

## What is on each tab

**Kolu overpass** — 45 keyframes, the survey clip. The baseline is fragmented and
holed; the rebuild is a closed surface. The number that matters is the last table row
pair: the feed-forward model put **0.00%** of its points more than 2.5 m above local
ground, on a scene whose structures are visibly several metres tall. That is the
"buildings are paint on a sheet" result in one figure.

**Village pass** — 42 keyframes, a portrait phone clip. Bigger detail gain than Kolu
(3.6× tighter at 6 cm) but **coverage goes the other way**: the rebuild holds only 83%
of the baseline's ground cells. Photometric MVS refuses surfaces it cannot match across
three views; the feed-forward model invents them. Both are the same trade. Only the
Kolu direction (136%) is quoted on the deck, so this tab is the honest other half.

**Village pass, whole clip** — the same source video ingested over its full 57 s
instead of the single moving pass. Keyframing found **2 shots** here against 1, spread
45 keyframes over 115 × 58 m, and produced a flat sheet: relief/footprint 0.037, 1.34%
of points above local ground. Kept because it is the failure that motivates adaptive
keyframing, and because the fix was choosing the span, not changing the model. No MVS
column — this run was never carried through the rebuild.

## Driving it

| key | does |
|---|---|
| `1` `2` `3` | switch clip |
| `b` | baseline / MVS rebuild |
| `m` / `c` | measure / clear |
| `p` / `v` | points / mesh |

Drag to orbit, shift-drag to pan, wheel to zoom. `measure` gives a straight-line
distance in metres between two clicked surface points, picked against the dense cloud.

## Where the numbers come from

The table is read at build time out of `out/ppt/measure_kolu.json` and
`measure_short.json`, written by `src/analysis/compare_mvs.py`. That script measures
relief **using one vertical taken from the baseline**, deliberately, so both columns
sit on the same axis. Do not substitute bounding-box extents off the packed meshes:
those use each model's own `upright_frame` and are quietly a different quantity.

"Surface residual, 6 cm neighbourhood" is the median distance from a plane fitted in a
6 cm ball. Lower is a tighter surface. The deck's "1.8–3.6× finer" is the ratio of
those two numbers across the two clips (1.8× Kolu, 3.6× Village).

## On the A/B toggle

The two models were packed independently, each normalised by its own extent, so the
page rescales both by metres-per-unit and subtracts each model's median centroid before
drawing. They are **not** re-registered to each other. Measured as-packed, their
footprints already overlap at IoU 0.69 (Kolu) and 0.64 (Village), and a best-fit yaw
search moved that by under 0.05 — there is no rotation worth correcting, and inventing
one would be fiction. If the two look slightly rotated on some views, that residual is
real and unfixed.

## Rebuilding

    python tools/build_gallery.py

Geometry is lifted straight out of each run's already-built `viewer.html`, so open3d is
not needed. The three clips are cut once and not regenerated by the build; the ffmpeg
commands are in the session that made them (Kolu is the same cut the demo uses, frames
781–1517).

## What it does not do

The 3D view does not fly along the video's camera path. `cam_centres.npy` holds
positions but no orientation, so a synced camera would mean inventing the rotation —
and an approximated flight path shown beside real footage would be the only simulated
thing on the page.
